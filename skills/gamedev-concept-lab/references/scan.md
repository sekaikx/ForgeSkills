# Store scan before inventing

Evidence rules (dated facts, verbatim quotes, confidence) are in the concept-judge skill:
`skills/gamedev-concept-judge/references/evidence.md`. Read that before filling rows.

Look up every row. This page has no prices and no "hot genre" list on purpose. A list
written into a skill becomes a stale opinion the next year.

## Search queries

Run several, not one:

- the verb the constraints allow ("defend at night", "carry loot home", "inspect and judge")
- that verb plus the store's tag for perspective (top-down, first-person), only if they
  fixed the camera
- the two closest games you already know, then the store's similar-games strip

Skip trailers. Open the store page and the review summary.

## Row

```
GAME | YEAR SHIPPED | PRICE as of DATE | REVIEWS (% positive) | VERB | REPEATED COMPLAINT (quote + url)
```

Eight rows minimum. If search returns only wishlists and no shipped game, say the demand
is unproven. Unreleased "coming soon" pages are not competitors with customers.

## How to call a verb crowded

Crowded means you filled three rows from the last 24 months with the same verb and a
review count large enough that strangers have finished it (hundreds, not a handful).
Write the three names. Do not say "the market is saturated" without them.

A crowded verb can still be a concept if the twist **changes what the player does**, and
you can point at a complaint in those reviews that the twist removes. "Nicer art" does
not remove a complaint about repetition.

## Sample bias

Recent reviews and angry threads over-represent the people a game failed. Note it. Do
not treat a Steam tag's player count, a view count, or a YouTube comment as buyers.

## What you are not doing

- Estimating revenue from review counts with a formula you did not cite this week.
- Copying a SaaS pricing ladder. Games are a one-time shelf price plus a discount later.
- Pitching live-service, ads, or battle passes. This library does not have a live-ops skill.
